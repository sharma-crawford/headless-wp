# Habeas Corpus: From Custody to Court

Research cutoff: October 7, 2026. Constantly changing law. Independent practitioner review pending.

## 1. What habeas corpus does

Ask the government to justify custody.

### Start with the person

A detained person’s first questions are often practical: Where am I? Why am I being held? Can someone help me challenge it? Habeas corpus is a judicial process for testing the legal basis for custody. The petition asks a court with authority over the case to examine detention and grant an appropriate remedy if the law requires one. It is a way to demand a lawful explanation and judicial review, not a promise that every detention ends. An advocate begins by listening, preserving the record, and connecting the person to qualified counsel.

- [28 U.S.C. §2241](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2241)
- [AILA: Why Every Immigration Attorney Should Consider Habeas in 2026](https://www.aila.org/library/think-immigration-why-every-immigration-attorney-should-consider-habeas-in-2026)

### The constitutional foundation

The Suspension Clause protects the privilege of the writ and specifies the exceptional conditions under which suspension may occur. Modern federal habeas authority also depends on statutes and precedent. The constitutional protection does not erase the rules governing custody, the right court, procedural requirements, or the claim being presented. The Latin phrase is commonly rendered “you have the body”; today the meaningful question is whether the government has lawful authority to restrain this person.

- [U.S. Constitution, Article I, §9, cl.2](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript)
- [28 U.S.C. §2241](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2241)

### Petition, writ, remedy

A petition is the request initiating the case. The court may order a response, evaluate facts and legal arguments, and deny or grant relief. A favorable decision may require release, a legally adequate custody hearing, or another tailored remedy. Winning access to a hearing is different from winning release; winning release from detention is different from winning an immigration status or defeating removal. Keep the requested remedy tied to the legal defect.

- [28 U.S.C. §2243](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2243)
- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)

## 2. Choose the right legal vehicle

Habeas is a family of procedures, not one universal form.

### Federal custody and immigration detention

Section 2241 authorizes specified custody challenges, including custody alleged to violate the Constitution, laws, or treaties of the United States. Immigration detention challenges commonly use this section. Custody is a legal requirement; present physical detention is the straightforward example, while other restraints and post-release cases require additional analysis. A volunteer’s role is to collect facts and identify urgency, not select and sign a petition as someone else’s lawyer.

- [28 U.S.C. §2241](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2241)
- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)

### State convictions and federal sentences

A person challenging custody under a state-court judgment generally proceeds under §2254, with its exhaustion and other restrictions. A federal prisoner attacking the validity of a federal sentence generally uses §2255 in the sentencing court, subject to its rules; its limited savings provision is not a free choice to use §2241 instead. State law may provide state habeas remedies too. This lesson’s district atlas addresses federal immigration detention, not a complete criminal postconviction guide.

- [28 U.S.C. §2254 (2024 edition; rules updated January 2025)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title28/html/USCODE-2024-title28-partVI-chap153-sec2254.htm)
- [28 U.S.C. §2255](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2255)

### Separate the claims

A challenge to the legal authority for confinement, a challenge to detention conditions, a challenge to a removal order, and a damages claim may require different vehicles and forums. Conditions claims are especially dependent on the requested relief and circuit law; do not assume every medical-care or facility complaint belongs in habeas. Counsel should classify each injury and requested remedy before combining claims.

- [28 U.S.C. §2241](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2241)
- [8 U.S.C. §1252](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1252)
- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)

## 3. Find the court and custodian

Location is a legal fact, not a detail.

### The usual starting point

For a core challenge to present physical custody, Rumsfeld v. Padilla supplies the default immediate-custodian and district-of-confinement rules. Start with the actual facility, its physical location, and the person who has day-to-day custody at the time of filing. The family’s home, counsel’s office, immigration court, ICE field office, and detention facility can be in different districts. Padilla expressly left an immigration-specific respondent question open; circuit law and particular facts must be checked rather than naming only a distant official by habit.

- [Rumsfeld v. Padilla, 542 U.S. 426 (2004)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-542/pdf/USREPORTS-542-426.pdf)
- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)

### Jurisdiction, venue, and transfer

Subject-matter jurisdiction concerns the court’s authority over this kind of claim. Jurisdiction over the custodian and territorial limits concern whom the court can reach. Venue concerns the proper location for the case. These issues interact but are not interchangeable. Record transfers with precise dates and times. A transfer before filing may change the right court; a transfer after a properly filed petition raises preservation and respondent questions that counsel must analyze. Do not assume transfers always destroy—or always preserve—jurisdiction.

- [Rumsfeld v. Padilla, 542 U.S. 426 (2004)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-542/pdf/USREPORTS-542-426.pdf)
- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)

### Why D.C. is not a universal forum

Naming federal officials in Washington does not make D.D.C. the right court for every detention case. In the Alien Enemies Act setting, Trump v. J.G.G. required the habeas challenge to be brought in the district of confinement and recognized notice and an opportunity to seek review before removal. That special statutory setting is distinct from ordinary INA removal-order review. Prompt qualified advice is crucial when transfer or removal is imminent.

- [Trump v. J.G.G. (April 7, 2025)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a931_2c83.pdf)
- [8 U.S.C. §1252](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1252)

## 4. Identify the detention statute

A custody challenge starts with the asserted source of power.

### Four common detention tracks

Section 1225(b) concerns applicants for admission and specified inspection/expedited-removal situations; its mandatory-detention provisions and parole mechanisms require careful classification. Section 1226(a) supplies discretionary pre-decision custody and potential bond or conditional parole. Section 1226(c) requires detention for enumerated categories; current statutory text must be read, including amendments, rather than reduced to “any conviction.” Section 1231 governs detention after the relevant removal order and removal-period triggers. These are starting categories, not a substitute for analyzing the actual custody and removal record.

- [8 U.S.C. §1225](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1225)
- [8 U.S.C. §1226](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1226)
- [8 U.S.C. §1231](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1231)

### Read the actual record

Obtain custody notices, warrants, bond decisions, the Notice to Appear, entry and parole documents, criminal records, removal decisions, appeals, stays, and prior removal/reentry history. Ask what provision DHS asserts and whether the facts fit it. A pending appeal or judicial stay can affect the analysis; a reinstated order and withholding-only proceedings are a distinct configuration. Guzman Chavez held §1231 applicable to the reinstated-order withholding-only situation before it.

- [Johnson v. Guzman Chavez (June 29, 2021)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-897_c07d.pdf)
- [8 U.S.C. §1231](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1231)

### The current interior-arrest dispute

In2026 appellate courts disagree about using §1225(b)(2)(A) for people already living in the United States without admission who are arrested in the interior. Several circuits reject that classification on the facts before them; Fifth and Eighth Circuit opinions accept it. Even a §1226 classification does not guarantee bond eligibility or release: subsection(c), flight risk, dangerousness, and other applicable rules may matter. Use the atlas to begin regional research.

- [Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, Supreme Court No. 26-104, docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-104.html)
- [8 U.S.C. §1225](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1225)
- [8 U.S.C. §1226](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1226)

## 5. Recognize a possible claim

Statutory authority and constitutional process are separate questions.

### Wrong authority or inadequate process

A statutory claim may argue that DHS is using the wrong detention provision or exceeds its authority. A constitutional claim may challenge prolonged detention or inadequate individualized process under the applicable due-process framework. Those claims require different legal analyses. Section 1226(e) limits review of discretionary judgments, but it is not a universal bar to challenges to statutory authority or constitutional legality. A district judge is not simply a second immigration judge deciding the same bond factors anew.

- [8 U.S.C. §1226](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1226)
- [Demore v. Kim, 538 U.S. 510 (2003)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-538/pdf/USREPORTS-538-510.pdf)
- [Jennings v. Rodriguez, 583 U.S. 281 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-583/pdf/USREPORTS-583-281.pdf)

### Do not turn six months into an automatic release date

Zadvydas interpreted §1231(a)(6) to avoid indefinite post-removal-period detention: six months is a presumptively reasonable period; after that, the person must give good reason to believe removal is not significantly likely in the reasonably foreseeable future, and the government must respond with sufficient evidence. Six months alone does not establish release. Jennings rejected reading automatic periodic six-month hearings into §§1225(b),1226(a),and1226(c), while leaving constitutional issues for further consideration. Arteaga-Martinez rejected a statutory six-month hearing requirement under §1231(a)(6); constitutional arguments remained distinct.

- [Zadvydas v. Davis, 533 U.S. 678 (2001)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-678.pdf)
- [Jennings v. Rodriguez, 583 U.S. 281 (2018)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-583/pdf/USREPORTS-583-281.pdf)
- [Johnson v. Arteaga-Martinez (June 13, 2022)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/19-896_2135.pdf)

### Build evidence for the actual theory

Preserve detention dates, reasons for delay, prior custody decisions, access to counsel, health concerns, the government’s removal efforts, relevant country/travel-document information, and the person’s goals. An advocate should record what is known and unknown without coaching or inventing facts. Exhaustion may be a judicially imposed requirement in §2241 detention litigation, with exceptions that vary; neither “always exhaust everything” nor “never exhaust” is reliable national instruction. Counsel must check the circuit and the particular administrative remedy.

- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)

## 6. Respect the limits on review

A detention petition is not a universal appeal from removal.

### The INA’s review channels

Section 1252(a)(5) generally makes a petition for review in the appropriate court of appeals the exclusive route for judicial review of a removal order. Sections1252(b)(9),1252(g),and the expedited-removal limitations can create further jurisdiction disputes. A genuine custody claim may be different from a disguised attack on removal, but courts disagree about some claims’ relationship to removal proceedings. Read the exact allegations and remedy—not merely the label “habeas.”

- [8 U.S.C. §1252](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1252)

### Expedited removal and special settings

Thuraissigiam rejected the attempted use of habeas in the setting before it to obtain additional asylum review rather than release from custody. Do not use that holding as a statement that every detention challenge is unavailable, or treat habeas as a routine appeal from a negative fear determination. Alien Enemies Act cases involve a different statute and urgent judicial-review questions. The jurisdiction and notice holdings of J.G.G. do not establish the merits of every AEA challenge.

- [DHS v. Thuraissigiam, 591 U.S. 103 (2020)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/19-161_g314.pdf)
- [Trump v. J.G.G. (April 7, 2025)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a931_2c83.pdf)

### Individual relief and class limits

Aleman Gonzalez interpreted §1252(f)(1) to restrict lower courts’ classwide injunctions against operation of covered INA provisions, while the statute has an individual-application exception. Do not infer from an individual victory that a nationwide injunction or an identical remedy for every detainee follows. Existing class orders require examination of class definition, jurisdiction, geographic scope, appeals, stays, and the relief actually in effect.

- [Garland v. Aleman Gonzalez (June 13, 2022)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/21pdf/20-322_m6hn.pdf)
- [8 U.S.C. §1252](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/8/1252)

## 7. Prepare a useful handoff

Accuracy and urgency belong in the same file.

### A practical advocate checklist

Confirm current custody location and safe communication; obtain informed permission to share records; record language and interpretation needs; create a dated custody and removal timeline; preserve actual notices and decisions; identify pending deadlines and possible imminent transfer/removal; document health and access concerns; connect the person to counsel qualified for the immigration and federal-court issues. Do not upload client details into this lesson. The learning tools store only quiz progress.

- [NILA habeas practice advisory, January 15, 2025, hosted by AILA](https://www.aila.org/aila-files/7904F590-529B-4A09-932D-DCC07F4E9FCB/25012900.pdf?1738163200=)
- [AILA: Why Every Immigration Attorney Should Consider Habeas in 2026](https://www.aila.org/library/think-immigration-why-every-immigration-attorney-should-consider-habeas-in-2026)

### What counsel must settle before filing

Counsel needs the claim, statutory and constitutional grounds, proper petition verification and petitioner/representative authority, respondent, district, exhaustion analysis, supporting evidence, requested remedy, local admission and filing rules, applicable form/fee or financial application, service process, redaction/sealing requirements, emergency-motion procedure, and current case status. AO242 is a national §2241 form; verify whether the selected court requires its own form or different instructions. A family relationship alone does not automatically qualify someone to litigate as next friend.

- [28 U.S.C. §2242](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/28/2242)

### Emergency relief is a separate request

Filing a habeas petition does not automatically stay removal or transfer. Counsel must analyze jurisdiction for the specific requested interim relief and use the applicable emergency procedure, with evidence of urgency and required notice/service. Section 2243 directs prompt action, but judicial scheduling and local orders determine actual response obligations; do not promise a hearing or release in a universal number of days. Preserve the court’s order and explain the next step accurately.

## 8. Read the district primer and recheck it

Today’s orientation is tomorrow’s research starting point.

### The current Supreme Court posture

On October 1, 2026 the Supreme Court granted certiorari in Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha, No. 26-104, concerning mandatory detention under §1225(b)(2)(A) of people present without admission. The grant is not a merits decision, not an automatic stay of all lower-court decisions, and not an instruction that everyone receives or loses bond. This edition’s cutoff is October 7, 2026 in America/Chicago. Review later orders and the docket before relying on the circuit map.

### A decision can stop being a usable rule

A panel opinion may be reheard, vacated, stayed, narrowed, or superseded. In the Fifth Circuit, the July 2, 2026 Sosnava Rodriguez panel’s constitutional 90-day hearing rule was vacated July 10 for en banc review; do not teach it as binding law. A certiorari petition is an advocate’s filing, not a Supreme Court holding. A judge-specific standing order is not automatically a districtwide rule. The atlas identifies these differences and records research limits.
